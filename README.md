# swift-acoustics-cavity

Use this package when a sound depends on the air enclosed in a body, such as a lute's bowl or a guitar's body with its rose or sound hole. Give `Acoustics.Cavity` the air as a volume of hexahedra or tetrahedra, with vertices in metres, and name the vertices where its pressure is released; every other boundary is a rigid wall. A body with an opening is given with some of the air outside it, whose far boundary is released. `Acoustics.Cavity.Modes` gives the lowest acoustic modes for an `Acoustics.Air`, each with its frequency, its pressure shape at every vertex and its modal volume. Compute them once and keep the value. `Acoustics.Cavity.Coupling` couples a closed cavity's modes to the walls that move, each an `Acoustics.Cavity.Wall` given by its modes in vacuo (a soundboard from swift-acoustics-plate, a bowl or a whole body from swift-acoustics-shell), and to its openings (`Acoustics.Cavity.Opening`, a sound hole or a rose), and gives the body's modes: frequency, mass, loss, the volume pushed out and each part's share. Struck at a vertex along a direction, a body mode is a resonator of mass `mass / r²` with r its `reach(at:along:)` there, which is the body's admittance at a bridge in modal form. The modes come from finite elements: the weak Helmholtz equation on swift-galerkin's trilinear hexahedra and linear tetrahedra, assembled into a sparse stiffness and mass of swift-sparse, whose lowest eigenpairs swift-eigen finds; the coupling integrals use swift-galerkin's surface Jacobian.

## Laws

- **Geometry.** A cell has eight corners in the order of swift-galerkin's `Galerkin.Reference.Hexahedron`, or four in the order of its `Tetrahedron`. A cell listed the other way round is turned. A cell with another number of corners, a corner that names no vertex, or a Jacobian that is not positive at every Gauss point fails with `.cell(index)`.
- **Physics.** The pressure obeys ∇²p + k²p = 0 with k = ω / c. A rigid wall is the natural boundary condition, ∂p/∂n = 0, and an open vertex holds p = 0. Air is lossless and its density does not enter the frequencies.
- **Modes.** The `count` lowest, ascending, a repeated frequency once per mode. A cavity with no open vertex has a first mode at zero frequency to rounding, whose shape is one everywhere and whose modal volume is the cavity's volume. Each shape is the pressure at every vertex, open ones zero, scaled so that its largest value is 1. `volume` is the modal volume at that scale, Λ = ∫ shape² dV. A wall moving with normal acceleration a drives the mode's amplitude q, the pressure q × shape, by q̈ + ω²q = −(ρc² / Λ) ∫ shape a dS. A cavity scaled by s in every length rings at 1/s the frequency, with s³ the modal volume. Frequencies are proportional to the speed of sound.
- **Coupling.** The air's modes and the walls' modes in vacuo are the basis. A wall's faces turn counterclockwise seen from outside the air. A wall mode's `displacement` is each vertex's motion per unit of its amplitude, and only its part along each face's normal, out of the air, moves the air, so where two faces of a wall meet at an angle, as a lute's top and bowl do at the rim, each moves as it should. The cavity's zero mode is a spring (ρc²/V)(Σ swept volume)² against everything that pushes air out; each other mode j adds the air's mass and its own resonance through Dₖⱼ = ∫ φₖ ψⱼ dS √(ρc²/Λⱼ)/ωⱼ, so the problem is symmetric, K = [Kʷ + K⁰, 0; 0, Ω²] and M = [Mʷ + DDᵀ, D; Dᵀ, I]. An opening's plug has mass ρ S L, L the wall's thickness plus 0.85a outside and inside (`Opening.circle`), and is driven by the uniform pressure only; a cavity with an open vertex has none and fails with `.opening`. A bad face or a displacement not given at every vertex fails with `.wall(index)`.
- **Body modes.** The `count` lowest, ascending. Each is scaled so that its largest wall-mode or plug amplitude is one, with `mass` at that scale and `volume` = Σ share × swept volume. `loss` is the walls' own loss factors weighted by the strain energy each holds, plus radiation as a simple source into free space, ρωV²/(4π m c). A uniform pressure only, one piston and one hole give Christensen and Vistisen's (1980) two oscillators exactly: their Eq. 8, f₊² + f₋² = f_p² + f_h² (Eq. 9), the plugged top's f_p² = f_p0² + ρc²A²/(4π² V m_p) (Eq. 4), and the admittance's zero at f_h (Eq. 6a). More air modes only lower a wall.
- **Bodies checked:** a 13.1 L guitar box from swift-mesh's `Volume(below:)` with an 88 mm hole: its held-wall air modes A1–A4 within 10% of Rossing's Table 3.1 (2010, p. 25) and its Helmholtz resonance, 133.9 Hz, within the 122–135 Hz Christensen and Vistisen measured; its lowest body mode breathes below both. A 10.1 L lute from `Volume(under:around:depth:cells:)`, barred as Firth's (1977): on a rigid bowl with a clamped spruce top its lowest admittance peak at the bridge, 138.5 Hz, is within 15% of Firth's 132 Hz air mode, but its top rings from 400 Hz up. As one swift-acoustics-shell body, a 2.5 mm top (Rossing 2010, p. 41) and 2.5 mm walnut ribs joined at the rim and held at a 2 cm neck block, with Firth's bars and a walnut bridge as bars of their true section, its peaks at 12 cells are 130 Hz for the air and 319, 369 and 582 Hz for the top, within 10% of Firth's 132, 304, 395 and 602 Hz; his 254 Hz has no top mode near it, and the nearest peak, 198 Hz, is the body rocking on its neck block. Holding the bowl as a wall of its own with the rim clamped moves no peak by 2%; joining it at the rim lowers the top's first by a fifth.
- **Element.** Consistent mass. The cube's Gauss rule is of degree 3 and the tetrahedron's of degree 2. Frequencies converge at second order in the cell size.
- **Checked against** closed forms:
  - **Rigid box**, 0.5 × 0.4 × 0.3 m: the first nine nonzero modes within 0.5% with hexahedra and within 2% with tetrahedra.
  - **Rigid cylinder**, radius 0.15 m and height 0.3 m: f = (c/2π)√((j′/a)² + (pπ/h)²), the first eight nonzero modes, pairs included, within 1%.
  - **Rigid sphere**, radius 0.2 m: f = c x / 2πa at the zeros x of jₗ′ for l = 1, 2, 0 and 3, sixteen modes with their multiplicities, within 1%.
  - **Helmholtz resonator**, a cylindrical body of radius 0.1 m and depth 0.1 m with a neck of radius 1 cm and length 2 cm, opening through a flange into air released 0.3 m away: within 2% of Ingard (1953, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 25, 1037), f = (c/2π)√(S / V L′), with the end corrections 0.85a outside and 0.85a(1 − 1.25a/R) inside. Extrapolated to no cell size, it is within 1%. The rim of the neck is singular, so cells must be fine there.
